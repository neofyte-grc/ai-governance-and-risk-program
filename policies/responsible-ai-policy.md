# Responsible AI Policy

## Peachtree Logistics Group

**Policy owner:** AI Governance Committee  
**Executive sponsor:** Chief Operating Officer  
**Status:** Draft portfolio artifact  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Effective date:** Upon approval  
**Review frequency:** At least annually and upon material change

---

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes mandatory principles and governance requirements for the responsible acquisition, development, configuration, use, monitoring, change, and retirement of artificial intelligence at Peachtree Logistics Group.

The policy is intended to ensure that PLG uses AI in a manner that is:

- Accountable.
- Human-centered.
- Safe and reliable.
- Fair.
- Secure.
- Privacy-preserving.
- Transparent.
- Traceable.
- Proportionate to risk.
- Consistent with approved business purposes.

---

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

- PLG employees.
- Contractors.
- Independent couriers.
- Temporary personnel.
- Consultants.
- Vendors and service providers.
- Internally developed AI.
- Third-party AI services.
- AI embedded in software, platforms, devices, analytics, or automated workflows.
- Experimental, pilot, and production AI use involving PLG business, personnel, customers, recipients, systems, or data.

Personal or publicly available AI tools are also subject to this policy when used for PLG work or with PLG information.

---

## 3. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Artificial intelligence | A machine-based system that generates predictions, recommendations, content, classifications, or decisions for defined objectives |
| AI system | The model, data, rules, software, interfaces, integrations, infrastructure, users, and business processes supporting an AI use case |
| AI use case | A defined business purpose for which an AI system is proposed or used |
| Affected party | A person or organization that may experience benefits, burdens, risks, or harm from an AI system |
| High-impact AI | AI capable of materially affecting safety, rights, employment, privacy, access, finances, services, or other consequential outcomes |
| Human oversight | Meaningful human review, challenge, approval, modification, rejection, escalation, or suspension of AI-supported action |
| Material change | A change that may affect purpose, risk, performance, data, users, affected parties, controls, or approval conditions |
| Generative AI | AI that generates text, images, audio, software code, or other content in response to user input |
| AI incident | An event involving actual or potential harm, misuse, control failure, security compromise, privacy loss, unfair impact, or unauthorized AI use |

## 4. Policy Principles

### 4.1 Accountability

A named business owner must remain accountable for every AI use case.

Responsibility for an AI-supported action may not be transferred to:

- The model.
- The algorithm.
- A vendor.
- A user interface.
- An automated workflow.

### 4.2 Human-centered use

AI must support people and approved business objectives.

AI must not remove required human judgment from consequential decisions unless the automated use has undergone specific assessment, testing, and authorization.

### 4.3 Safety and resilience

AI systems must be designed and operated to reduce foreseeable harm.

High-impact systems require stronger:

- Testing.
- Human oversight.
- Monitoring.
- Fallback.
- Incident response.
- Governance approval.

### 4.4 Fairness

PLG will evaluate whether AI produces unjustified disparities or harmful outcomes.

Relevant analysis may consider:

- Workload.
- Opportunity.
- Access.
- Scheduling.
- Route burden.
- Service quality.
- Complaints.
- Errors.
- Other context-appropriate outcomes.

### 4.5 Privacy

AI data use must be:

- Necessary.
- Authorized.
- Minimized.
- Protected.
- Retained appropriately.
- Limited to approved purposes.

### 4.6 Security

AI systems, integrations, credentials, data, outputs, logs, and administrative functions must be protected from unauthorized access, alteration, disclosure, destruction, and misuse.

### 4.7 Transparency

Users and decision-makers must receive relevant information concerning:

- AI involvement.
- Intended purpose.
- Known limitations.
- Material inputs.
- Recommendation basis.
- Uncertainty.
- Human-review requirements.
- Available escalation and correction options.

### 4.8 Validity and reliability

AI performance must be evaluated for the intended operating context.

Performance must be monitored and reassessed when data, models, vendors, users, or operating conditions change.

### 4.9 Traceability

PLG must retain sufficient records to reconstruct material AI-supported decisions.

Records should include relevant:

- Inputs.
- Versions.
- Outputs.
- Human reviews.
- Overrides.
- Downstream actions.
- Outcomes.
- Incidents.
- Corrective actions.

### 4.10 Lifecycle governance

AI authorization is conditional.

PLG may restrict, suspend, roll back, or withdraw approval when:

- Risks increase.
- Controls fail.
- Performance degrades.
- Harm occurs.
- A material change is unapproved.
- Required evidence becomes unreliable.

## 5. AI Inventory and Registration

AI use cases must be registered before:

- Acquisition.
- Development.
- Connection to PLG systems.
- Testing with PLG or sensitive data.
- Pilot use.
- Production use.

The inventory record must include:

- System and use-case identifiers.
- Business and technical owners.
- Purpose.
- Capabilities.
- Users.
- Affected parties.
- Data categories.
- Vendor and dependencies.
- Impact tier.
- Lifecycle stage.
- Approval status.
- Review and retirement information.

Unregistered or shadow AI use is prohibited.

---

## 6. Impact and Risk Assessment

AI use cases must undergo risk-based impact screening.

The assessment must consider:

- Safety.
- Rights.
- Workforce effects.
- Privacy.
- Security.
- Data quality.
- Fairness.
- Transparency.
- Reliability.
- Human oversight.
- Legal and contractual obligations.
- Third-party dependencies.
- Operational continuity.
- Reputation and stakeholder trust.

High-impact AI requires documented:

- Risk assessment.
- Risk treatment.
- Controls.
- Human oversight.
- Evaluation.
- Monitoring.
- Incident response.
- Governance approval.

Risks must be reassessed when a material change occurs.

---

## 7. Human Oversight

Consequential AI-supported decisions require meaningful human oversight appropriate to their potential impact.

Reviewers must have:

- Appropriate training.
- Authorized access.
- Relevant contextual information.
- Sufficient review time.
- Clear decision authority.
- The ability to modify or reject AI output.
- Escalation and fallback procedures.
- Protection from retaliation for good-faith challenge.

Human approval must not become a ceremonial or automatic step.

---

## 8. Data Governance

AI data must have:

- An approved source.
- A documented purpose.
- An accountable owner.
- Appropriate classification.
- Documented lineage.
- Quality and freshness requirements.
- Retention and deletion requirements.

Only necessary data may be processed.

Personnel must not enter personal, confidential, regulated, or sensitive information into unapproved AI services.

AI-influenced outcomes must be identified so that they are not automatically treated as independent ground truth in future training or evaluation.

---

## 9. Evaluation and Testing

AI systems must be evaluated before pilot or production authorization.

Evaluation must address applicable areas such as:

- Functional accuracy.
- Data quality.
- Safety.
- Fairness.
- Privacy.
- Security.
- Explainability.
- Human oversight.
- Reliability.
- Resilience.
- Foreseeable misuse.
- Failure and recovery.

Acceptance criteria must be approved before formal testing.

Failed mandatory criteria must block deployment unless the affected use is removed or the issue is remediated and retested.

---

## 10. Monitoring and Reporting

Production and pilot AI systems must have ongoing monitoring appropriate to risk.

Monitoring may include:

- Performance.
- Data quality.
- Drift.
- Safety.
- Assignment outcomes.
- Human-review behavior.
- Overrides.
- Complaints.
- Incidents.
- Availability.
- Vendor performance.
- Control effectiveness.

Threshold breaches must be routed to an accountable owner and may trigger:

- Investigation.
- Corrective action.
- Revalidation.
- Restriction.
- Rollback.
- Suspension.

---

## 11. Third-Party AI

AI vendors must undergo risk-based due diligence before contract execution or connection to PLG systems.

Vendor agreements should address:

- Approved purpose.
- Data ownership.
- Data use and reuse.
- Security and privacy.
- Subprocessors.
- Model and service versions.
- Material-change notification.
- Performance and availability.
- Incident notification and cooperation.
- Audit and evidence rights.
- Data return and deletion.
- Portability.
- Transition and termination.

Vendor responsibility does not replace PLG accountability.

---

## 12. Change Management

Material AI changes require assessment, testing, approval, and revalidation before deployment.

Changes must be versioned and traceable.

PLG must maintain:

- Change records.
- Impact assessments.
- Regression-test results.
- Approvals.
- Deployment evidence.
- Monitoring.
- Rollback capability.

---

## 13. Incident Reporting

Personnel must promptly report suspected:

- Harmful output.
- Unsafe reliance.
- Unauthorized AI use.
- Security events.
- Privacy events.
- Unfair impact.
- Human-review bypass.
- Logging failure.
- Unapproved change.
- Out-of-scope use.
- Monitoring or fallback failure.

PLG may restrict, suspend, disconnect, roll back, or retire an AI system while an incident is investigated.

---

## 14. Prohibited Practices

PLG personnel must not:

- Use AI for an unregistered or unapproved purpose.
- Allow prohibited autonomous action.
- Bypass mandatory human review.
- Use AI output as the sole basis for employment discipline or termination without approved governance and lawful process.
- Use protected characteristics or unjustified proxies to create harmful outcomes.
- Enter credentials, payment data, PHI, personal records, confidential contracts, or restricted information into an unapproved service.
- Conceal material AI use when disclosure is required.
- Disable logging, safeguards, monitoring, or escalation.
- Ignore a stop condition or formal suspension.
- Represent unverified AI output as confirmed fact.
- Use PLG data for unrelated model training without authorization.

---

## 15. Governance and Risk Acceptance

The AI Governance Committee will establish:

- Lifecycle gates.
- Approval requirements.
- Risk tolerance.
- Exception authority.
- Reporting expectations.
- Suspension and retirement decisions.

Risk-acceptance requirements are:

| Risk level | Required authority |
| --- | --- |
| Low | Authorized risk owner |
| Moderate | AI Governance Committee |
| High | Chief Operating Officer after committee review |
| Critical | Outside normal appetite; avoid, redesign, restrict, or discontinue |

Risk acceptance must be:

- Documented.
- Time-limited.
- Conditional.
- Monitored.
- Assigned to an accountable owner.
- Reconsidered upon material change.

---

## 16. Exceptions

Exceptions require:

- The requirement being excepted.
- Business rationale.
- Scope.
- Risk assessment.
- Compensating controls.
- Accountable owner.
- Approval authority.
- Effective and expiration dates.
- Monitoring requirements.

No exception may waive applicable law, safety requirements, contractual duties, or executive authority.

---

## 17. Training and Awareness

Personnel must receive training appropriate to their AI responsibilities.

Training may include:

- Approved and prohibited use.
- Data handling.
- AI limitations.
- Human oversight.
- Automation bias.
- Fairness.
- Security.
- Privacy.
- Incident reporting.
- Escalation.
- Change management.
- Vendor responsibilities.

High-impact users must demonstrate competency before access.

---

## 18. Enforcement

Violations may result in:

- Access removal.
- Corrective training.
- Investigation.
- Contractual action.
- Disciplinary action.
- System restriction or suspension.
- Vendor escalation.
- Other appropriate response consistent with PLG policy and applicable requirements.
